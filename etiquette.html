Etiquette

In a Japanese supermarket, one person may scan your shopping while you pay at a machine and pack your own bag.

Shopping in a store

Say you pick up a snack in Nagoya. Here’s how to get from the shelf to the shop door. The checkout setup can vary even between branches of the same chain.

In the Philippines In Japan
1. Pick up the item
In a self-service shop, take the item from the shelf, check its price in pesos and put it in your basket. Use a store basket and keep the package sealed until you’ve paid. If the shelf tag shows two prices, 税込 marks the price including tax. For a collectible behind glass, point it out to a staff member.
2. Find the end of the line
You may be used to picking an open cashier lane and lining up behind the last shopper. Some stores have one shared queue. Look for floor arrows or a queue sign before choosing a register. If everyone is in one line, join the back and wait for the next cashier to call you forward. If each register has its own line, pick an open lane.
3. Set down your basket
At a staffed lane, place your goods on the counter or belt for the cashier. If there’s a bagger, you can give them your reusable bag. Set your basket on the counter. If the cashier scans your items into a second basket, leave the first basket with the cashier. Show your tote or ask for a bag now. Many shops charge for one. At a self-checkout, scan each item yourself.
4. Choose how to pay
Check the peso total. Tell the cashier if you’re paying cash, by card or with GCash where it’s accepted. Check the total in yen (¥). Even if a cashier scanned everything, you may pay at a screen beside you or at a numbered machine they point to. Pick a method shown there. Cash, card, transit IC and QR options vary by store.
5. Make the payment
For cash, hand over your pesos or use the tray if provided. For a card or QR payment, follow the terminal or app prompt and wait for confirmation. For cash, use the tray if the cashier sets one out. At a machine, choose Cash and insert yen in the marked bill and coin slots. For a card, choose Credit Card, then tap or insert as prompted. For a QR wallet, check for its logo and follow the screen.
6. Collect your change and receipt
If the total is ₱180 and you hand over ₱200, collect ₱20 in change. Take your receipt and your card if you used one. If the total is ¥680 and you insert ¥1,000, collect ¥320 in change. Check the coin tray, bill outlet and receipt slot before stepping away. If you inserted a card, take it when prompted.
7. Pack up and return the basket
If a cashier or bagger packed your purchase, collect your bags. When you pack your own, use the space the store provides. After paying, take the basket with your groceries to the bagging table and pack your bag. Stack the empty basket with the others, usually beside the table or near the exit. At a convenience store, if staff keep the basket and hand you your purchase, take your items and receipt and you’re done.
