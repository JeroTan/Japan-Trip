Etiquette

Compare two everyday routines with the Philippines. Start with Shopping in a store or jump to Riding trains.

Shopping in a store

Say you pick up a snack in Nagoya. Here’s how to get from the shelf to the shop door. The checkout setup can vary even between branches of the same chain.

In the Philippines In Japan
1. Pick up the item
In a self-service shop, take the item from the shelf, check its price in pesos and put it in your basket. Use a store basket and keep the package sealed until you’ve paid. If the shelf tag shows two prices, 税込 marks the price including tax. For a collectible behind glass, point it out to a staff member.
2. Find the end of the line
You may be used to picking an open cashier lane and lining up behind the last shopper. Some stores have one shared queue. Look for floor arrows or a queue sign before choosing a register. If everyone is in one line, join the back and wait for the next cashier to call you forward. If each register has its own line, pick an open lane.
3. Set down your basket
At a staffed lane, place your goods on the counter or belt for the cashier. If there’s a bagger, you can give them your reusable bag. Set your basket on the counter. If the cashier scans your items into a second basket, leave the first basket with the cashier. Show your tote or ask for a bag now. Many shops charge for one. At a self-checkout, scan each item yourself.
4. Choose how to pay
Check the peso total. Tell the cashier if you’re paying cash, by card or with GCash where it’s accepted. Check the total in yen (¥). Even if a cashier scanned everything, you may pay at a screen beside you or at a numbered machine they point to. Pick a method shown there. Cash, card, transit IC and QR options vary by store.
5. Make the payment
For cash, hand over your pesos or use the tray if provided. For a card or QR payment, follow the terminal or app prompt and wait for confirmation. For cash, use the tray if the cashier sets one out. At a machine, choose Cash and insert yen in the marked bill and coin slots. For a card, choose Credit Card, then tap or insert as prompted. For a QR wallet, check for its logo and follow the screen.
6. Collect your change and receipt
If the total is ₱180 and you hand over ₱200, collect ₱20 in change. Take your receipt and your card if you used one. If the total is ¥680 and you insert ¥1,000, collect ¥320 in change. Check the coin tray, bill outlet and receipt slot before stepping away. If you inserted a card, take it when prompted.
7. Pack up and return the basket
If a cashier or bagger packed your purchase, collect your bags. When you pack your own, use the space the store provides. After paying, take the basket with your groceries to the bagging table and pack your bag. Stack the empty basket with the others, usually beside the table or near the exit. At a convenience store, if staff keep the basket and hand you your purchase, take your items and receipt and you’re done.

For store-specific examples, see 7-Eleven Japan’s payment screens, JNTO’s etiquette guide and this supermarket checkout walkthrough.

Riding trains

Yes, Japan has elevated trains like Manila’s MRT/LRT. Some tracks run on viaducts above streets and shops, as you can see around Ueno in Tokyo. Other trains run at street level or underground. Your Nagoya → Sakae ride is underground on the yellow Higashiyama Line. Think of LRT-2’s Katipunan station: the platform is below the street, and you still enter through gates and choose a direction. See your hotel-to-Sakae route.

First ride? Follow the steps from your Nagoya hotel to Sakae and back.

What does the station jingle mean?

In Nagoya, listen as the train approaches. The yellow Higashiyama Line plays “Dream” for trains toward Fujigaoka, the direction from Nagoya to Sakae. On your way back, “Yellow Line” plays for trains toward Takabata. Check the destination display, wait behind the platform line and let passengers step off first. Choose a city sound file (WMV). “Dream” toward Sakae · “Yellow Line” for the return. Nagoya subway melody guide.

On some JR East platforms in Tokyo, the melody signals departure. Boarding is finishing. If the doors are closing, let that train go and wait for the next one. The display, announcements and staff tell you which train to take. JR East on departure bells · Tokyo Metro platform safety (PDF).

Can I pay for one ride with my credit card?

Yes, for your Nagoya subway ride you can tap a supported contactless bank card at a marked gate. If you want a regular paper single ticket from the subway machine, bring yen cash. Each adult needs their own card or ticket.

  • Nagoya → Sakae, or a Tokyo Metro ride. If your Visa or Mastercard has contactless enabled for overseas use, look for its logo on the gate. Use that reader and skip the ticket machine. Tap in and tap out with the same physical card or the same phone. Even if the phone holds that card, stick to whichever you used to enter. For a paper single at either subway’s ordinary machine, choose English, select your destination or fare, insert yen, and collect your ticket and change. Nagoya → Sakae is ¥210 one way per adult. Nagoya bank-card gates · Nagoya machine rules · Tokyo Metro bank-card gates · Tokyo Metro paper tickets.
  • Centrair Airport → Meitetsu Nagoya. A contactless card such as Visa or Mastercard can cover the base fare at the marked airport gate and at Meitetsu Nagoya’s Central or West gate. For a paper ticket by credit card, use the airport’s Meitetsu Travel Plaza. The ordinary ticket machines take cash or stored transit credit. Choose an ordinary car for the base fare alone. For μSKY, buy the separate reserved-seat μticket before boarding, even if you tapped in. Airport ticket options · Eligible stations and gates.
  • Nagoya → Tokyo by Shinkansen. Buy this intercity ticket separately. At JR Nagoya Station you can pay by credit card at the JR ticket office, or book ahead on the SmartEX website. If you link a manaca to an already-paid SmartEX reservation, the card opens the gate for that booking. JR ticket offices and machines · SmartEX boarding guide.

Want something like a beep card? Get a manaca.

In Manila you load pesos onto beep. In Nagoya you load yen onto manaca and tap it the same way. Get one Japanese transit IC card per person. Keep it for eligible local rides in Tokyo, including Tokyo Metro and JR East’s Suica area. For your JR Keiyo Line trip to Hatchobori, use manaca or a JR ticket. Tokyo Metro’s bank-card tap service covers a different network. Where manaca works.

  1. Buy one when you land. From Centrair T2, follow the train signs to Access Plaza. At Central Japan International Airport Station, the Meitetsu counter and ticket machines are on the left of the gates. Ask for an anonymous adult manaca, or use a machine marked for manaca sales. Bring ¥2,000 in cash for each card at Meitetsu. That gives you ¥1,500 to spend and a ¥500 deposit. Airport station layout · Meitetsu card sales.
  2. Or buy it at Nagoya subway station. At a manaca sales machine choose English, then the manaca purchase option, Unregistered (anonymous), and an amount. Insert cash and take the card and change. A staffed subway gate window can also sell one. City’s machine guide.
  3. Reload when your balance runs low. At a compatible machine choose Charge, place or insert your card as shown, select an amount, and insert yen cash. Take your card and check the new balance. Manaca top-up guide.

From the station entrance to the exit

In the PhilippinesIn Japan
1. Find your line and direction
On MRT-3 you might look for “toward Taft Avenue” or “toward North Avenue” to choose the correct platform. Same idea. Check the operator, line color and last station on the sign. For Sakae, board at Nagoya H08 toward Fujigaoka. Fushimi is the first stop and Sakae H10 is the second. Follow elevator signs if stairs are difficult.
2. Enter the fare gate
Tap your loaded beep card on the reader. For one MRT-3 ride, select your destination at the machine or ask the teller. Tap the physical single-journey ticket to enter and keep it for the exit. With manaca, Suica or PASMO, tap the IC pad and wait for the gate to open. With a subway paper ticket, insert it in the slot and pick it up again as you walk through. For a bank card, use the marked bank-card reader.
3. Queue and board
Stand behind the platform line. Leave the doorway clear so passengers can get off before you board. Do the same here. Follow the floor markings for each train door, stand to the side, and let people get off first. On board, silence your phone, keep conversations low and leave priority seats available for people who need them.
4. Get off and settle the fare
Tap beep again to exit and keep the card. With an MRT-3 single-journey ticket, insert it in the exit gate. Tap out with the same card or device. For a completed subway trip, the exit gate keeps your single paper ticket. If the gate signals an error, step aside and show your ticket or card to staff. For a low IC balance, use the fare-adjustment or recharge machine before exiting.
